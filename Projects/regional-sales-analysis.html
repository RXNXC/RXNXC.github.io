02 analytical questions

Questions to Be Answered

Four analytical questions were defined upfront to give the analysis clear direction. Each maps to a specific business concern — revenue performance, salesforce quality, promotion efficiency, or operational logistics.

Q1

Which region generates the highest total revenue, and how does customer type (Retail vs. Wholesale) influence that breakdown?

Q2

Which salesperson has the highest sales volume and the lowest return rate?

Q3

Do promotions actually increase order value, or do discounts just erode revenue?

Q4

What is the average delivery time by region, and which region has the most delayed orders?

03 data cleaning

Data Cleaning Process

The raw dataset arrived as a single sheet with 1,500 rows and 19 columns. The following steps were applied before any analysis to ensure accuracy and consistency across all formula outputs and pivot summaries.

01
Date column conversion The Date, OrderDate, and DeliveryDate columns were stored as Excel serial numbers (e.g. 44980). Converted to readable date format via Format Cells → Date and cross-verified against the DeliveryDays column to confirm internal consistency before any date subtraction was performed.
02
TotalPrice formula audit TotalPrice was verified against Quantity × UnitPrice × (1 − Discount). Floating point artifacts (e.g. 2290.4000000001) were identified as rounding byproducts and left intact — pivot aggregations handle them correctly.
03
Binary column check (Returned, Discount) The Returned column was confirmed to contain only 0 and 1 values — no nulls or unexpected entries. Discount values ranged 0–0.15 (0%–15%) with no anomalies. Both columns were clean and ready for COUNTIFS and AVERAGEIF use.
04
Categorical field standardization Region, Product, CustomerType, PaymentMethod, Promotion, DeliveryStatus, and StoreLocation were inspected for typos or inconsistent casing using COUNTIF and Filter. All values were consistent — no corrections required.
05
Duplicate OrderID check OrderIDs (REG100000 onwards) were checked for duplicates using COUNTIF. No duplicates found across all 1,500 rows.
06
Null / blank cell scan Full dataset scanned using Go To Special → Blanks. One trailing unnamed blank column was identified and deleted. No missing values found in any analytical column.
07
Derived column: Delivery Days A new Delivery Days column was created as =DeliveryDate − OrderDate, then formatted as Number (not Date). Without this step, Excel renders the result as a date serial — day 7 appears as '07/01/1900' instead of the integer 7. This column is the analytical foundation for all Q4 calculations.

04 metrics & rationale

Metrics Used Per Question & Why

Q1

Revenue by Region & Customer Type

Total Revenue per Region

=SUMIF(Region_Column, RegionName, TotalPrice_Column)

Sums TotalPrice for every row where Region matches the target. SUMIF is dynamic — results auto-update if source data changes. TotalPrice is used (not Quantity or UnitPrice) because it already accounts for both quantity and discounts — it reflects true realized revenue.

Revenue Split by Customer Type

=SUMIFS(TotalPrice_Column, Region_Column, RegionName, CustomerType_Column, "Retail")

SUMIFS matches Region AND CustomerType simultaneously. Retail and Wholesale have fundamentally different purchasing behaviour — aggregating them together hides which segment drives each region's performance.

% of Grand Total

=Region Total Revenue / Grand Total Revenue

Knowing North contributes 22.1% is more actionable than knowing it generated $967,958. It also guards against misleading comparisons when regions have different order volumes.

Visualisation

chart

Clustered Bar Chart — shows absolute revenue values side-by-side per region, placing Retail and Wholesale in one view. Horizontal bars are easier to read with region labels on the Y-axis.

Q2

Salesperson Sales Volume & Return Rate

Total Revenue & Order Count per Salesperson

=SUMIF(Salesperson_Column, Name, TotalPrice_Column) =COUNTIF(Salesperson_Column, Name)

Revenue captures both frequency and order size. Order count reveals workload and is the denominator for Return Rate — without it the rate cannot be calculated.

Return Rate

=COUNTIFS(Salesperson_Column, Name, Returned_Column, 1) / COUNTIF(Salesperson_Column, Name)

Normalises across reps with different volumes. A rep with 64 returns from 243 orders (26.3%) is performing worse than one with 64 returns from 259 orders (24.7%) even though raw counts are identical. Without normalisation, high-volume reps always appear to have "more" returns.

Revenue & Return Rate Rank

=RANK(Revenue, Revenue_Range, 0) // descending — rank 1 = highest revenue =RANK(ReturnRate, ReturnRate_Range, 1) // ascending — rank 1 = lowest return rate

Ranks convert both metrics to a common 1-to-6 ordinal scale. The ascending sort on Return Rate is deliberate — a lower rate is better, so rank 1 must go to the smallest value.

Visualisation

chart

Combo Chart (Column + Line) — revenue and return rate are on different scales (hundreds of thousands vs. a decimal). A secondary axis on the line prevents either metric from distorting the other.

Q3

Promotion Effectiveness

Average Order Value (AOV) per Promotion

=AVERAGEIF(Promotion_Column, PromoName, TotalPrice_Column)

The most important metric here. Total revenue could be high simply because a promotion ran on more orders — not because it drove higher spend. AOV isolates the per-transaction effect.

Revenue Lift vs. No-Promotion Baseline

=Promo AOV − No_Promotion AOV

The direct answer: is this promotion worth running? Positive lift means customers spend more per order than they would without any promotion. Negative lift means the discount costs more than the incremental revenue it generates.

Average Discount Rate & Quantity

=AVERAGEIF(Promotion_Column, PromoName, Discount_Column) =AVERAGEIF(Promotion_Column, PromoName, Quantity_Column)

Discount rate is the cost side. Average quantity distinguishes volume behaviour (more items per basket) from value behaviour (higher spend per item), separating a successful volume promotion from a margin-eroding discount.

Visualisation

chart

Clustered Column Chart — AOV and Avg Discount side-by-side per promotion makes the cost-benefit relationship immediately visible without needing a secondary axis.

Q4

Delivery Time & Delay Rate by Region

Delivery Days (derived column)

=DeliveryDate − OrderDate → formatted as Number, not Date

Not in the original dataset. Excel stores dates as serial numbers so subtraction gives exact days elapsed. Critical: format result as Number or Excel renders it as a date serial (7 appears as '07/01/1900').

Average & Maximum Delivery Days per Region

=AVERAGEIF(Region_Column, RegionName, DeliveryDays_Column) =MAXIFS(DeliveryDays_Column, Region_Column, RegionName)

Average captures typical experience. MAXIFS identifies worst-case — if all regions share the same max (10 days), the ceiling is a system constraint, not a regional failure. MAXIFS is preferred over MAX+IF array formula for performance.

Delay Rate

=COUNTIFS(Region_Column, RegionName, DeliveryDays_Column, ">"&OverallAvg) / COUNTIF(Region_Column, RegionName)

"Delayed" = above the overall average (6.04 days) — a data-driven threshold, not arbitrary. The dynamic reference auto-updates if data changes. Rate (not raw count) normalises for regions with different order volumes.

Visualisation

chart

Combo Chart (Bar + Line) — delivery days and delay rate are on different scales. Bars on the primary axis and a line on the secondary axis let both metrics be read simultaneously.

06 formula reference

Formula Choice Summary

Every formula was chosen to answer a specific business question accurately, dynamically, and without manual recalculation. The guiding principle: condition on exactly what the question asks, return a normalised output, and stay robust when source data changes.

QuestionPrimary FormulaWhy This Formula
Q1 – Revenue by RegionSUMIF / SUMIFSConditional aggregation across one or two dimensions; auto-updates on data change
Q1 – % of Grand TotalDivisionNormalises regional revenue to a comparable share regardless of order volume
Q2 – Salesperson RevenueSUMIFSame conditional logic as Q1 applied to the Salesperson dimension
Q2 – Order CountCOUNTIFActivity metric and denominator for Return Rate
Q2 – Returned OrdersCOUNTIFSMulti-condition count: Salesperson match AND Returned = 1 simultaneously
Q2 – Return Rate & RankDivision + RANKNormalises across volumes; RANK converts to comparable ordinal scale
Q3 – Avg Order ValueAVERAGEIFPer-transaction average isolates the promotion's spending effect
Q3 – Revenue LiftSubtraction vs baselineQuantifies net impact vs. no-promo baseline; positive = worth running
Q3 – Avg Discount & QtyAVERAGEIFCost side and volume behaviour; separates value from volume promotions
Q4 – Delivery DaysDate SubtractionDerived metric; must format result as Number not Date
Q4 – Avg Delivery DaysAVERAGEIFBounded 0–10 day distribution makes average appropriate; conditions on Region
Q4 – Max Delivery DaysMAXIFSOutlier check; preferred over MAX+IF array formula for clarity
Q4 – Delay RateCOUNTIFS + DivisionData-driven threshold (6.04 day avg); rate normalises for regional volume

07 recommendations

Recommendations

Based on the four analytical questions and their findings, the following recommendations are offered to improve revenue performance, reduce return costs, and address delivery inefficiencies.

// action items
  • Prioritise FREESHIP over discount-based campaigns. FREESHIP generated the highest orders, highest AOV (+$51.47 lift), and highest total revenue — all while carrying the same 7% average discount as WINTER15. Weight free shipping over percentage discounts, especially for Wholesale customers whose order values justify the logistics cost.
  • Discontinue or restructure WINTER15. It is the only promotion producing a negative revenue lift (−$13.26 vs. no-promo baseline). Customers spend less on WINTER15 orders than they would without any promotion — the discount erodes value without driving compensating volume.
  • Audit North region logistics. With a 52.75% delay rate — the worst of any region — North's supply chain needs a targeted operational review. Despite being the top revenue region, this delay frequency is a retention and satisfaction risk at scale.
  • Review Carlos's sales process. Carlos has both the highest return rate (28.15%) and the most orders (270), producing 76 returned orders — the most of any rep. A review of product-segment fit and targeted coaching could meaningfully reduce costly returns and protect margin.
  • Benchmark against Alice. Alice ranks #2 in revenue and #2 in return rate quality — the best balance of volume and sales quality. Her approach should be documented and shared with lower-performing reps, particularly Carlos and Bob.
  • Investigate North's delay-return correlation. North holds both the highest delay rate (52.75%) and highest return count (88). If further analysis confirms delays are driving returns — not just correlated — logistics investment in North could reduce both metrics simultaneously, creating compounding ROI.
