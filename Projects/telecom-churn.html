01 tools used

Tools Used

The entire analysis was built inside Microsoft Excel — cleaning, aggregation, profiling, cross-tabulation, and visualization all contained in a single workbook any stakeholder can open without additional software.

Microsoft Excel Primary environment — all cleaning, analysis, and dashboards in one self-contained workbook
Pivot Tables Aggregated customer counts, churn rates, and profile averages across every analytical dimension — Status, Contract, Internet Type, Revenue Quartile, Offer
Pivot Charts Bar charts, combo charts, pie and donut charts, and line charts driven directly by Pivot Table outputs and connected to slicers
Cross-Tabulations Two-way frequency tables converted to row-percentages to compute churn rate by segment — the core methodology for identifying structural churn risk factors in Q3
Slicers Interactive filters connected to all Pivot Tables and Charts, enabling real-time segmentation by Customer Status, Contract type, Internet Type, and Offer
Conditional Formatting Colour scales and data bars applied to Pivot Table outputs to surface outliers, highest-risk segments, and top churn reasons at a glance without additional charts

03 data cleaning

Data Cleaning Process

The dataset (7,042 rows, 38 columns, 267,596 cells) had 30,844 missing values across 15 columns. Before any analysis, every column was audited to distinguish genuine data quality gaps from structurally meaningful blanks — where a blank encodes real information rather than absence of data. Every missing-value pattern traced to a clear structural cause. No rows and no columns were dropped from the working dataset.

Column(s)Missing% MissingRoot Cause
Churn Category, Churn Reason 5,17373.5% Structurally blank for Stayed and Joined customers — only meaningful for churned records
Offer 3,87655.0% Customer received no promotional offer — absence is itself a meaningful business state
Internet Type, Avg Monthly GB Download, 8 add-on service columns 1,52621.7% Customer has no internet service — sub-service fields are not applicable
Multiple Lines, Avg Monthly Long Distance Charges 6819.7% Customer has no phone service — fields are not applicable
01
Churn Category & Churn Reason — conditional retention with fill Both columns were retained and filled with "Not Churned" for Stayed and Joined records when analyzing the full customer base. When analyzing churn drivers (Q3), the dataset was filtered to Customer Status = Churned, leaving both columns 100% populated. Dropping either column would have removed the only direct evidence of stated churn reasons; dropping all rows with any blank would have removed 73.5% of the dataset — entirely eliminating the Stayed and Joined groups needed to answer Q1 and Q2.
02
Offer — filled with explicit "No Offer" category Missing Offer values were filled with "No Offer" rather than with the mode or left blank. A missing value here means the customer was not given a promotional offer — itself a meaningful and common business state affecting 55% of customers. Treating it as its own category preserves this signal for acquisition analysis (Q1) and ensures the "No Offer" group is represented in Pivot Table outputs rather than excluded.
03
Internet Type & 8 add-on columns — structural "No Internet Service" fill Internet Type was filled with "No Internet Service"; Avg Monthly GB Download with 0; all 8 internet add-on columns (Online Security, Online Backup, Device Protection, Premium Tech Support, Streaming TV, Streaming Movies, Streaming Music, Unlimited Data) with "No Internet Service." Cross-tabulation confirmed that all 1,526 missing rows correspond exactly to customers where Internet Service = No. These blanks are not data gaps — they are the expected absence of sub-services when the parent service is absent. Filling preserves row completeness without fabricating values.
04
Multiple Lines & Long Distance Charges — structural "No Phone Service" fill Multiple Lines was filled with "No Phone Service" and Avg Monthly Long Distance Charges with 0. Identical logic to the internet group: all 681 missing rows correspond to customers where Phone Service = No — confirmed by cross-tabulation. A customer without phone service cannot have multiple lines or long-distance charges, so these fills are factually correct, not estimated.
05
Post-cleaning validation After applying all fills, the full dataset was re-scanned for unexplained nulls. Cross-tabulations (Internet Type × Internet Service; Multiple Lines × Phone Service; Churn Category × Customer Status) were re-run to confirm every fill aligned 1:1 with its parent flag before any Pivot Table was built. Zero unexplained blanks remained in any column used in the analysis.

04 metrics per question

Metrics Used Per Question & Why

Metrics were selected to be directly interpretable by a non-technical audience while remaining methodologically sound. The single most important methodological choice throughout this analysis is using churn rate (%) rather than raw churned counts whenever comparing segments — without this, larger segments always appear riskier simply because they contain more customers.

Q1 Customers joined last quarter
Count of Customer Status = "Joined" The dataset's native outcome label — the most direct, auditable count without building a derived proxy. No explicit join-date column exists, so Tenure in Months ≤ 3 was used as the "last quarter" proxy and validated by confirming all Joined customers fell within that range, making the proxy internally consistent rather than an assumption layered on top of data.
Net customer change (Joined − Churned) Raw joined count without context hides whether the company is growing or shrinking. Netting against churned customers gives the single most actionable figure for the business: whether the customer base expanded or contracted in the period. 454 joined vs. 1,869 churned is a meaningfully different picture than "454 new customers."
Offer distribution within Joined group Identifies which promotions, if any, are driving new acquisition — directly informing future marketing spend allocation. If only one offer converts new customers, that is the only offer worth expanding for growth purposes.
Q2 Customer profiles by status
Avg Age, Married %, Avg Dependents Demographic anchors that describe who each group is — revealing whether household structure and life stage correlate with retention. Means used rather than medians because these fields are close to symmetric or are percentage fields where the mean is the standard and most interpretable summary for business stakeholders.
Avg Tenure in Months The strongest loyalty indicator in the dataset. Long tenure means the customer has repeatedly renewed; short tenure means they are still evaluating the service. The gap between churned (18 months) and stayed (41 months) is the sharpest single profile difference found — a 2.3× difference that signals tenure is the most predictive observable attribute for future churn risk.
Avg Monthly Charge & Avg Total Revenue Monthly Charge captures current price point; Total Revenue captures full lifetime contribution. Using both together distinguishes a short-tenure high-payer from a long-tenure moderate-payer — two different retention risk profiles that appear identical on Monthly Charge alone. The fact that churned customers pay the most per month ($73.35) while generating the least lifetime value ($1,971) is only visible when both metrics are shown.
% on Month-to-Month contract The most operationally actionable profile attribute — the company can directly influence contract type through incentives. Surfacing that 88.6% of churned customers are on month-to-month contracts (versus 32.8% of Stayed customers) directly connects the "who churns" finding to "what the company can change."
Q3 Key drivers of churn
Churn Category & Churn Reason frequency (%) These are the only fields capturing stated cause — what the customer or retention agent recorded at cancellation. Frequency as a percentage of all churned customers (not raw count) is used so that rarer categories can be compared fairly to larger ones. These answer "what customers say drove their decision" — necessary but not sufficient on its own, since customers may not always articulate the real driver.
Churn rate by Contract type Tests whether contractual commitment level predicts churn independently of what customers stated. Churn rate (not raw churned count) is essential: month-to-month is the most common contract type, so a raw count comparison would overstate its risk purely because its denominator is larger. The rate-based comparison reveals the true 17× gap between month-to-month (45.8%) and two-year (2.6%) contracts.
Churn rate by Internet Type Internet Type is a service-quality proxy. A fiber optic churn rate that significantly exceeds DSL or cable — despite fiber being a premium product — suggests a price-to-performance perception gap. This is a driver customers may not explicitly state but that shows up strongly in the behavioral data, complementing the stated-reason analysis.
Churn rate by Premium Tech Support status Tests whether support access functions as a retention lever and quantifies the effect size. A large gap provides a direct business case for widening support access as a retention investment — and the 26-point gap (41.6% without vs. 15.2% with) is large enough to justify that investment without further statistical testing.
Q4 High-value customer loss
Total Revenue quartiles (Q1–Q4) Total Revenue (not Monthly Charge) was used to define "high value" because it reflects realized lifetime contribution, not just current pricing — a customer who has paid for five years is more valuable than one paying a higher rate for one month. Quartiles (not a fixed dollar threshold) were chosen so "high value" is always defined relative to this specific customer base, making the definition robust if re-run on a different period or population.
Churn rate within each revenue quartile Determines whether churn risk is concentrated in lower-value or higher-value customers. If high-value customers churn less frequently but their revenue impact per loss is disproportionately large, both facts need to be visible for a complete picture. The monotonically declining churn rate (45.3% → 14.8% from Q1 to Q4) confirms that value and loyalty co-occur in this dataset.
Avg Monthly Charge & Avg Tenure for Q4 churned Characterises exactly who was lost among high-value churners: what they were paying ($91.58/month) and how long they had stayed (54.9 months average) before leaving. A nearly 5-year average tenure before churn directly signals the company had a long intervention window but did not act proactively — this is the most actionable finding in Q4.
Churn Reason frequency within Q4 churned The top-quartile group may leave for different reasons than the overall churned population — determining this decides whether a retention strategy can be uniform or must be tiered. The overlap (devices, offers, support) with the overall population suggests the same drivers apply across value segments, making a single retention programme more feasible.

06 recommendations

Recommendations

The following recommendations are derived directly from the data findings and prioritized by effect size — the largest measured impact first.

// action items · ranked by effect size
  • 01Contract migration incentives — highest leverage
    Moving a customer from month-to-month to a two-year contract is associated with a churn-rate reduction from 45.8% to 2.6% — the single largest effect found in the analysis. Since 88–90% of both churned and newly joined customers sit on month-to-month plans, even a modest shift in contract mix would produce a material reduction in overall churn. Targeted migration offers (e.g., one free month on a 12-month commitment) should be the first priority.
  • 02Device upgrade programme for long-tenure customers
    "Competitor had better devices" is the single most common stated churn reason and the top reason among high-value churned customers. A subsidised device upgrade path triggered at a tenure milestone — for example 24 months — directly targets the largest stated cause of loss without requiring pricing changes or contract restructuring.
  • 03Proactive retention offers before the competitor window
    "Competitor made a better offer" is the second-largest stated reason (311 customers). Because Offer E is the only acquisition-linked promotion and all other offers appear tied to existing customers, current outreach is reactive. Triggering a personalised retention offer for customers entering the 18–24 month tenure band — before competitor outreach typically occurs — would address this earlier in the customer lifecycle.
  • 04Expand Premium Tech Support coverage and improve quality
    Customers without Premium Tech Support churn at 41.6% versus 15.2% for those with access — a 26-point gap. "Attitude of support person" is the third most common churn reason overall (220 customers) including 28 high-value churners. Two separate problems require distinct interventions: a coverage gap (not enough customers have access) and a quality gap (the experience itself drives departures).
  • 05Early-warning monitoring for high-value, long-tenure accounts
    High-value churned customers stayed for an average of 54.9 months before leaving — a nearly five-year window where proactive retention could have intervened. A monitoring flag for top-quartile revenue customers entering a month-to-month renewal window, or showing declining service engagement, would convert this from a reactive loss into a manageable retention opportunity.
