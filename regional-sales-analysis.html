Regional Sales
Analysis

A full analytical deep-dive into 1,500 sales orders across 5 regions, 8 product types, 4 store locations, and 6 salespersons — uncovering revenue patterns, delivery performance, and return drivers to guide operational decisions.

Dataset 1,500 orders
Columns 21 fields
Regions East, West, North, South, Central
Total Revenue $4,379,992
Deliverable Excel Workbook + Dashboards

02 analytical questions

Questions to Be Answered

Six analytical questions were defined upfront to give the analysis direction. Each question maps to a specific business concern — revenue performance, operational efficiency, or customer behavior.

Q1

Which region, store location, and product generate the most revenue? How does revenue split between Retail and Wholesale customers?

Q2

Which salesperson drives the most revenue and orders? Are there performance gaps between salespersons?

Q3

Which promotions attract the most orders and revenue? Do higher-discount promotions actually produce more revenue?

Q4

What is the overall delivery performance? Which regions experience the most delays, and how long does delivery typically take?

Q5

What is the product return rate across regions, customer types, and salespersons? Are returns concentrated in specific segments?

Q6

Is there a relationship between delay rate and return rate by region? Do regions with worse delivery also see more returns?

03 data cleaning

Data Cleaning Process

The raw dataset arrived as a single sheet with 1,500 rows and 21 columns. Before any analysis, the following cleaning steps were applied to ensure accuracy and consistency across all pivot tables and calculations.

01
Date column conversion The Date, OrderDate, and DeliveryDate columns were stored as Excel serial numbers (e.g. 44980). These were converted to readable date format using Excel's Format Cells → Date, and cross-verified with the DeliveryDays column to confirm internal consistency.
02
TotalPrice audit The TotalPrice field was verified against the formula: Quantity × UnitPrice × (1 − Discount). Floating point precision issues (e.g. trailing decimals like 2290.4000000001) were identified as rounding artifacts and left as-is since pivot aggregations handle them correctly.
03
Boolean column check (Returned, Discount) The Returned column contained only 0 and 1 values — no nulls or unexpected entries. Discount values ranged from 0 to 0.15 (0%, 5%, 10%, 15%) with no anomalies. Both columns were confirmed clean.
04
Categorical field standardization Region, Product, CustomerType, PaymentMethod, Promotion, DeliveryStatus, and StoreLocation were inspected for typos or inconsistent casing using COUNTIF and Filter. All values were found consistent — no correction needed.
05
Duplicate OrderID check OrderIDs (REG100000 to REG101000+) were checked for duplicates using COUNTIF. No duplicate IDs were found in the 1,500-row dataset.
06
Null / blank cell scan The full dataset was scanned using Go To Special → Blanks. One trailing blank column (unnamed) was found and deleted. No missing values were found in any analytical column.

04 metrics & rationale

Metrics Used Per Question & Why

Q1

Revenue by Region, Store & Product

MetricWhy it was used
SUM(TotalPrice)Aggregated revenue is the primary performance indicator for regional and product comparisons. Using TotalPrice (post-discount) reflects actual realized revenue, not list prices.
Revenue split by CustomerTypeRetail vs. Wholesale have different price sensitivities and margin structures. A split reveals whether volume or value drives each region's revenue.
Revenue by StoreLocationIdentifies which physical stores contribute most — helps with resource allocation and underperforming location decisions.
Q2

Salesperson Performance

MetricWhy it was used
COUNT(OrderID) per SalespersonVolume tells you who is closing the most deals — useful for assessing activity and pipeline management.
SUM(TotalPrice) per SalespersonRevenue quality matters more than volume. A salesperson closing fewer but higher-value orders may be more strategic than one with high volume but low value.
Return Rate per SalespersonHigh returns associated with a specific salesperson may signal mismatched customer expectations, overselling, or poor product fit — a quality metric beyond pure revenue.
Q3

Promotion Effectiveness

MetricWhy it was used
COUNT(OrderID) per PromotionOrder volume per promo indicates reach and uptake — which promotions attracted the most purchases.
SUM(TotalPrice) per PromotionRevenue per promotion shows whether the discount cost was offset by volume gains. High order count but low revenue signals over-discounting.
Avg. Discount & Avg. Quantity per PromotionHelps understand the discount-to-quantity relationship — are customers buying more per order when discounts are higher?
Q4

Delivery Performance

MetricWhy it was used
Delay Rate = Delayed / Total OrdersA normalized rate (not raw count) enables fair comparison across regions with different order volumes.
Avg. Delivery Days per RegionReveals how long customers wait on average — a proxy for logistics efficiency and customer experience.
Max Delivery DaysFlags worst-case delivery experiences, which drive the most customer dissatisfaction even when averages look acceptable.
Q5

Return Rate Analysis

MetricWhy it was used
Return Rate = Returned / Total OrdersNormalized rate removes volume bias — a region with 500 orders and 100 returns is a bigger problem than one with 50 returns out of 1,000.
Return Rate by SalespersonSurfaces whether returns cluster around specific reps, pointing to behavioral or process issues that can be corrected through training.
Return Rate by RegionGeographic patterns in returns may signal product-market fit issues, delivery damage, or quality problems specific to certain supply chain routes.
Q6

Delay Rate vs. Return Rate Correlation

MetricWhy it was used
Delay Rate & Return Rate side-by-side per RegionPlacing both rates in the same view allows visual pattern-matching — if high delay regions also have high returns, it suggests delivery experience is driving dissatisfaction and returns.
Scatter comparison (Delay % vs. Return %)A side-by-side table with both rates per region is the simplest way to test whether a correlation exists without needing statistical software.

Q3 — Promotion Effectiveness

FREESHIP outperforms SAVE10 and WINTER15 in both orders and revenue — buyers appear to value delivery savings over price cuts, a critical insight for promotion strategy.

North

06 recommendations

Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the following recommendations are offered to improve revenue performance, reduce return costs, and address delivery inefficiencies.

// action items
  • Prioritize FREESHIP promotions over discount-based promos. FREESHIP generated the most orders and highest revenue. Future campaign spend should weight free shipping over percentage discounts, particularly for Wholesale customers whose order values justify the cost.
  • Investigate and address North region delivery delays. With a 52.8% delay rate — the highest across all regions — the North logistics chain needs audit. Despite high revenue, this delay rate is a retention risk as customer expectations tighten.
  • Review Carlos's sales process to reduce return rates. Carlos has the highest return rate at 28.1%. A review of which products and customer types he is primarily selling to, and a targeted coaching session, could reduce costly returns and protect margin.
  • Investigate South's elevated return rate independent of delivery. South has the highest return rate (26.1%) but not the highest delay rate — meaning the cause is likely product quality, incorrect expectations, or order accuracy issues. A root cause analysis focused on returned product categories in the South is recommended.
  • Monitor Phone category performance. Phones are the lowest-revenue product ($497K) with a large gap from the top-three products (~$684K). Understanding whether this is a pricing, demand, or placement issue could unlock underutilized revenue.
  • Leverage Store D's performance model. Store D leads all locations in revenue ($1.2M). Identifying what Store D does differently — in terms of staffing, promotions, or product mix — and replicating it across underperforming stores could lift aggregate revenue.
